### 1. "See the stimuli better on the dual panel"

Only the geometry contract changes (drawing box and minimum ink width); renderers, seeds and replay specs are untouched, and the bitmap up-scaling of tiny objects (one coin, one stroke) is capped at 1.8× their native ink so small values are enlarged by the bigger drawing box rather than blurred. Measured on desktop (350 px halves): grid 258 → 295 → 322 px wide; a vertical ten-frame 335 → 490 → 536 px tall.

### 2. "Level up manually"
The level card (menu, Staircase and Rating modes, once the relational phase has started) now has **manual steps**: ▼/▲ for the **relational tier** (1–14) and ▼/▲ for the **META spacing** (0–5). A forced tier step does everything Alberto's own level change does (level, peak, reading-range sync, adaptive-baseline shape, snapshot invalidation, prepared-trial refresh) plus the fork's bookkeeping (window reset, rating nudged into the new tier's band, logged as manual). The normal rules keep adapting afterwards — a tier that turns out too hard steps back down on its own. Baseline keeps Alberto's rules untouched, so the buttons are disabled there with a hint. Rationale in the card: self-chosen difficulty produced the same gains and transfer as adaptive difficulty in the controlled comparisons (von Bastian & Eschen 2016; Lau et al. 2025).
Also fixed on the way: the level card now refreshes when the menu opens through the pause button / Escape (the emergency-menu path defers `updateMenu`).

The card collapses/expands (remembered per profile). The block summary now ends with **"Next: x% to LV n · <first open condition>"** and a **🏁 Level milestone** line when a 10-level mark was crossed.

### 3. Reaction time, fairly ("mine is always red")
The old tile pooled every trial into one average and coloured it against a single target, so anyone who reads long questions was "slow" every block. Now:
- **Per-trial budget** = the trial type's target (L0 1.8 s, META¹ 3.8 s, META² 6.5 s, META³ 9 s) **+ 220 ms per word of question beyond the first eight** (question + half the option text). A 40-word nested question gets ~7 s of reading allowance.
- The tile shows the **median RT per trial type** (L0 first, META layers after), the share of correct answers **within budget**, and **your usual** median for that type; the colour compares you with your own pace (good if ≥ 65 % in budget or ≤ 1.05× usual; bad only if < 40 % in budget and > 1.3× usual).
- Per-trial feedback: a correct answer slower than the raw target but within budget shows a green ✓ instead of grey.
- **Rating mode** uses the same reading allowance in its speed–accuracy score, so long questions no longer cost rating.
(Staircase mode never used RT for progression; Baseline still uses Alberto's RT rule, as designed.)

### 4. Small things
- The MEM banner for retained META answers ("REMEMBER THIS ANSWER · MEM² 1/2 · for META²") no longer overflows on phones.
- Learn: vocabulary note in the META lesson (pair total = LEFT + RIGHT; "difference between the two pair totals" is |(L1+R1) − (L2+R2)|; "all four retained slots" = L1, R1, L2, R2 — Dark's own mix-up), level-card and RT-tile explanations in Levels/Controls, About card updated.

## v1.2 — the transfer & mastery layer

Built from the literature review (progression methods with evidence for performance and transfer). The honest summary of that review:
near transfer is real, far transfer to general ability is not established (Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016; Sala et al. 2019), and the
one training family with long-term real-world outcome data is adaptive, speeded, divided-attention **speed-of-processing training with boosters**
(ACTIVE: Rebok et al. 2014; Edwards et al. 2017; Coe et al. 2026). So v1.2 does two things: it trains the same processes in **as many variations
as possible** and keeps difficulty just above threshold without pinning you there (mastery, plateau-breaking), and it adds the ingredients with the
best transfer evidence (variability + measured generalisation, spacing across days + boosters, divided attention, variable priority) — every one a
toggle in **Settings → Progression → Transfer & mastery** (Staircase and Rating modes; Baseline stays Alberto's untouched rules).

| Toggle (default) | What it does | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Short staircases** (on) | Every block opens with four easy lead-in trials (exposure ×2, ×1.7, ×1.45, ×1.2 back to threshold; ×1.6→×1.1 in numerical sense), ~20 % of the other trials are clearly suprathreshold (×1.5), and each visual format carries its own exposure offset on top of the global staircase (−0.02 log units per correct, +0.08 per error, clamped ×0.74…×1.57), so slow-read formats get more time and fast ones less. Exposure never exceeds the mode's start value. | Multiple short staircases with easy trials transferred where a single at-threshold staircase did not (Hung & Seitz 2014); easy-to-hard enables generalisable learning (Ahissar & Hochstein 1997); per-format staircases avoid roving interference (Kuai et al. 2005). |
| **Transfer probes** (on) | Two formats are held out of relational training (rotating order per profile). Every 6th relational block is a **🧪 PROBE** block on those formats only (HUD tag, block-intro tag). Transfer index = probe accuracy − trained accuracy (last 60 non-probe L0 trials). In Staircase mode a probe lagging by more than 10 points **holds the tier** for up to 12 blocks and tightens the coverage cap; probe trials never move the tier. Hold-outs rotate every 40 relational blocks or at the end of a course ("Rotate now" button too), so every format is trained over time. Prepared trials are invalidated at probe boundaries so formats do not leak across. | Variability → generalisation (Raviv, Lupyan & Green 2022); transfer follows shared processes and must be measured, not assumed (Dahlin et al. 2008; Simons et al. 2016). |
| **Format coverage** (on) | No format above ~15 % of the last 100 relational trials (10 % while a probe holds the tier); formats you read worse or have not seen for days are drawn more often (weight = (1 + 2·(1−acc)) · (1 + staleness) / (1 + 4·share)). Implemented on top of Alberto's own family balancing, which already boosts weak and absent question families. | COGITO / heterogeneous training (Schmiedek et al. 2010; Karbach & Kray 2009); supply–demand mismatch needs demand that one routine cannot meet (Lövdén et al. 2010). |
| **Focus blocks** (on) | Every 4th relational block alternates **⚡ SPEED** (correct answers score 60–100 by speed against a 3.6 s deadline, errors 0) and **🎯 ACCURACY** (errors count double: score = 100·(correct − wrong)/n). HUD/intro tag and a scored summary line. | Variable-priority / emphasis-change training (Gopher, Weil & Bareket 1994; Kramer, Larish & Strayer 1995; Boot et al. 2010). |
| **Divided-attention marker** (off, experimental) | UFOV-style: a small **▲** flashes with the quantity at one of eight fixed peripheral spots in the frame margins (corners and mid-edges — the regions stimuli never fill); after the mask, dashed spots appear: tap where the ▲ was, then answer the question. 20-trial windows: ≥ 85 % → marker level up (smaller, dimmer: 11 → 9 → 7 px), then 2 and 4 ■ distractors; < 60 % → down. Misses add exposure time (+0.08 log units, hits −0.015, ≤ ×1.8). Scored per block ("Marker 17/20"). Adds tap time to RT, so compare RTs only with it on or off. Works for single-stimulus and LEFT/RIGHT flashes; not for DOM-rendered spatial trials. | ACTIVE speed training = central identification + peripheral localisation + distractors, adaptive (Ball et al. 2002; Rebok et al. 2014; Edwards et al. 2017; Coe et al. 2026); pacing/divided attention as "learning to learn" ingredients (Bavelier & Green 2019). |
| **Calendar & boosters** (on) | Training days, minutes and blocks per day; 10 training days = a **course**; booster suggestions at +4 and +12 weeks (4 sessions each); soft daily cap (25 min) and "six days in a row" / "no training this week" nudges; shown in Settings and as a line in the block summary. Nothing is locked. | Spacing across days (Cepeda et al. 2008; Wang, Zhou & Shah 2014); home training > 3×/week ineffective in Lampit et al. 2014; ACTIVE boosters. |

Also in v1.2:
- **Staircase tier rule is now chance-corrected**: Σ(correct − chance)/Σ(1 − chance) over the 20-trial window must reach 0.70 (≈ 85 % on YES/NO, ≈ 77 % on 4-way choice, 70 % on typed numbers); down below 0.20. Wilson et al.'s 85 % rule concerns binary discriminations; open answers get the lower raw target the Math Garden / geography-system trials favour (Jansen et al. 2013; Papoušek, Stanislav & Pelánek 2016).
- **META spacing re-justified**: what matters is absolute spacing, not the expanding shape (Karpicke & Bauernschmidt 2011) — text in About/Learn corrected.
- **Learn**: new lesson 7 "Training for transfer" (what the evidence supports, each toggle explained with live status, a routine that fits the evidence, what the numbers cannot tell you); "Levels" and "Controls" lessons updated (block tags, marker usage); About card lists the layer and the references.
- Settings status shows the held-out formats, the last probe, the slowest/fastest format offsets, the marker level and the calendar (today, week, course, boosters).

### What was deliberately not built (and why)
- **Relational-complexity META axis** (ternary/quaternary META questions): would need new question specs inside Alberto's generator/registry (~900 specs with review text and audits) — too invasive for a patch layer; the Learn chain demo covers the idea. Candidate for a base-version change.
- **Bayesian (QUEST/Psi) exposure thresholds**: better for measurement, but concentrating trials at threshold is exactly what the short-staircase evidence argues against for training.
- **Expectation priming, gamification for its own sake, self-controlled difficulty as a learning booster**: unsupported or ethically dubious (Foroughi et al. 2016 did not replicate; Katz et al. 2014; McKay et al. 2022).

### META¹ says which values to use
Under every META¹ question: "Use the values **as shown** for both pairs" or "Use → Pair 1: **TRANSFORMED** values · Pair 2: values **as shown**".

### Progression modes (Settings → Progression)
| Mode | Tier rule | Level shown |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | Alberto's v7.1.22 within-individual baseline, untouched (RT-improvement gate is what holds people at a tier) | LV1–LV14 (tier) |
| **Staircase** (default) | Windowed accuracy at the current tier: up after a 20-trial window ≥85 % (families gate kept), down if the last 12 < 60 %. No RT gate. | LV 1–100 = 45 % tier + 20 % exposure + 20 % META layer + 15 % spacing |
| **Rating** | Elo/IRT rating θ (start 1060). δ = 800 + 55·tier + {0,230,420,600}[layer] + learned per-family offset. Tier = highest with expected success ≥ 80 % (±15 hysteresis). Score: correct 0.75–1.0 by speed, wrong 0–0.2 by speed (deadlines 3.6/7.6/13/18 s). K 40→24→14, item K 8. | LV = 1 + (θ−1040)/10, rating shown next to it |

Post-cap "runway" bands (LV9–14 in tier terms) keep Alberto's META-evidence blockers. Exposure keeps Alberto's weighted up-down staircase in all modes.

### Adaptive META spacing (Staircase and Rating)
Spacing level s = 0…5. After 4 consecutive correct META answers s+1; after 2 wrong within the last 4, s−1. Interference lures between MEM 1/2 and 2/2 with lag ⌈s/2⌉…s; exactly s ordinary trials between the last retained item and the question. No new chain opens while a question is held.

### Continuous META chain (eto's suggestion, toggle, on in the new modes)
When a META layer is genuinely active, a chain opens on the next eligible trial instead of by probability, and a new one opens right after a META is delivered. Applies to Staircase and Rating.

### Frameworks
Wilson, Shenhav, Straub & Cohen (2019) 85 % rule · Levitt (1971) / Kaernbach (1991) staircases · Landauer & Bjork (1978) expanding retrieval · Klinkenberg, Straatemeier & van der Maas (2011) Math Garden Elo · Maris & van der Maas (2012) speed-accuracy scoring · Ball et al. (2002) UFOV exposure staircase.
